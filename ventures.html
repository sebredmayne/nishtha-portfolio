FILE 05 · CATEGORY STRATEGY

Category @ Mosaic

Two months, one product: NutriMix 2+. My job was to scale it — which mostly meant getting a lot of parents to a maybe.

WHERE
CEO's office, Mosaic Wellness
ROLE
category strategy
TIME
2 months
I sell
the maybe.

An ad rarely makes the sale. Its job is to get someone to a maybe — a click, an add-to-cart. That was my job for two months on NutriMix 2+.

01  STOP SCROLLINGthe hook
02  MAYBEadd-to-cart
03  YEScheckout team's job
my
job
WHAT I WAS GRADED ON
ROAS — the report card. Did the ad money come back?
Add-to-cart — the situationship of commerce. Not a yes. A maybe.
Everything in between: which ads ran, which got cut, and which story was doing the work.
WHAT I TESTED + BUILT
STORY
the calcium gap, taste, natural ingredients, no sugar
FORMAT
creator UGC, music reels, voiceovers
LANGUAGE
English, Hindi
REGIONAL
designed regional product pages and ran regional ads to go with them, so the ad and the page speak the same language
CONFIDENTIAL
relative numbers only
WHAT THE BEST ADS TAUGHT ME NutriMix · three of the best-performing Meta ads, each compared with the group's median
01 · CHEAPEST CUSTOMER ~17% ↓ below the median cost of a new customer. Not the loudest ad — a music reel about the calcium gap, the quiet worry that your kid might be missing something. Most people paid upfront, too.
02 · BEST RETURN ~1.3× the median return. A creator just talking about taste and natural ingredients. The most people got to maybe here (highest add-to-cart), and the most money came back.
03 · ATTENTION ≠ BUYERS ~3× → 0.6× The Hindi back-to-school voiceover was the best whisper of the lot: ~3× the click-through, 2× the hook. Then nobody stayed for the countdown — ~0.6× the return.

The takeaway: getting someone to stop scrolling is easy. Getting them to the cart is the job. The ad people stopped for wasn't the ad they bought from.

three ads is a tiny sample — patterns, not proof

Now I leave things in my own cart overnight.
